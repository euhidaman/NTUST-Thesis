# CLAUDE.md — Writing Style Instructions

## Language and Tone
- Write in clear, precise academic English
- Use words that mean exactly what you intend — avoid vague filler words like "various", "numerous", "significant", "straightforward", "robust", and "novel" unless they are genuinely the right word in context
- Prefer specific, concrete language over generic academic padding
- Do not use "leverage" as a verb when "use" or "apply" is more direct
- Do not use "utilize" when "use" suffices
- Avoid "in order to" — write "to" instead
- Avoid "due to the fact that" — write "because"
- Do not start sentences with "It is worth noting that" or "It should be mentioned that" — just say the thing

## Punctuation Rules
- **Never use em dashes** (— or --) anywhere in the document, in any context
- Use commas, colons, semicolons, or restructure the sentence instead
- If you feel the urge to use an em dash, either split it into two sentences or use a comma

## Academic Writing Standards
- Define every acronym on first use
- Introduce every figure, table, and equation in prose before it appears
- Every mathematical symbol must be defined in the sentence or equation where it first appears
- Do not use bullet points in body prose — use numbered lists only for contributions in the introduction, and full sentences everywhere else
- Write in third person
- Do not use contractions

## What to Avoid
- Filler transitions: "In this section, we will discuss..." — just start discussing
- Redundant phrases: "past history", "future plans", "end result"
- Vague quantities: "many", "a lot of", "quite" — use specific numbers where possible
- Overpromising: "state-of-the-art", "groundbreaking", "revolutionary" — let the results speak
- Hedging without reason: "somewhat", "relatively", "rather"
